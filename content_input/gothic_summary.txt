    <tr>
      <td><strong>Part I: Arrival at High Place</strong><br>(Chapters 1–6)</td>
      <td>Twenty-two-year-old Mexico City socialite Noemí Taboada receives a frantic, incoherent letter from her newlywed cousin Catalina, claiming her English husband Virgil Doyle is poisoning her and that the walls are whispering. Noemí's father sends her to El Triunfo, Hidalgo, to investigate. Arriving at High Place—an isolated, mold-covered Victorian mansion shrouded in mountain fog—Noemí meets the cold matriarch Florence, the quiet nephew Francis, and the ancient patriarch Howard Doyle, who expresses racist eugenicist views.</td>
      <td>Contrast between cosmopolitan 1950s Mexico City modernity and isolated British colonial aristocracy; enforced household silence; Victorian atmospheric decay.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Part II: The Looming Gloom</strong><br>(Chapters 7–15)</td>
      <td>Noemí visits Catalina, who drifts between lucidity and wild terror, begging Noemí to get herbal medicine from the local curandera, Marta Duval. Noemí explores the abandoned silver mine and the hilltop English cemetery with Francis. Noemí suffers vivid sleepwalking episodes, nightmares of a golden woman, and visions of glowing yellow mold spreading through the wallpaper. When Noemí attempts to take Catalina home, Virgil and Dr. Cummins lock down the estate under the pretext of tuberculosis quarantine.</td>
      <td>Gaslighting and mid-century psychiatric control of women; eugenic theories of bloodline purity; empirical mycology and biological body horror replacing traditional ghosts.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Part III: The Mycological Secret</strong><br>(Chapters 16–22)</td>
      <td>Francis reveals the family's secret: the Doyles' wealth and unnatural lifespans depend on an ancient European symbiotic fungus ("the Gloom") cultivated in the cemetery soil. Howard lives for centuries by absorbing healthy hosts through blood marriages. Howard reveals his plan: because the family bloodline has degenerated through incest, Noemí must be forcibly married to Francis to infuse fresh vitality into their fungal collective. Noemí is forced to drink a fungal tea during a terrifying initiation ceremony.</td>
      <td>Body horror and loss of physical consent; biological exploitation as an allegory for imperial extraction of Mexican silver; hereditary incest as monstrous eugenics.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Part IV: The Final Rupture</strong><br>(Chapters 23–27)</td>
      <td>Noemí uses Marta Duval's herbal tincture to fight off the fungal control. Francis defies his family and helps Noemí rescue Catalina. In the crypt, Noemí discovers the mummified remains of Agnes, the original fungal matriarch. Catalina stabs Virgil to death. Noemí sets the underground mycelium and the mansion on fire. As High Place burns to the ground, Howard and Florence are consumed by the inferno. Noemí, Catalina, and a wounded Francis escape into the dawn.</td>
      <td>Destruction of the colonial patriarch; reclamation of female bodily autonomy; physical collapse of the haunted house archetype; liberation from ancestral trauma.</td>
    </tr>
