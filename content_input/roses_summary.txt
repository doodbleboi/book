      <td><strong>Chapters 1–5</strong><br>(pp. 1–40)</td>
      <td>Anna Klein eats a spoonful of lard for breakfast and brushes her teeth with her finger. She cuts the toes off six-year-old Amalia's shoes so they still fit. Amalia finds a heart-shaped metal dirndl button in the street rubble on Adolfsallee. Anna leaves Amalia on a bench outside the Wiesbaden Landesmuseum to type custody forms. Captain Henry Cooper scolds Anna for leaving her child alone, but he gives Amalia a copy of <em>The Snow Queen</em>. Anna and Amalia eat salty beef bouillon in the Wilhelmstrasse mess hall. Amalia gets sunstroke on the bench. Plumber Emil Schilling introduces Anna to his sister Frieda, who offers childcare in exchange for food rations at their Gustav-Freytag-Strasse villa with hot apple strudel and Nescafé.</td>
      <td>Immediate post-war survival; strict ration cards; de-Nazification questionnaires (<em>Fragebogen</em>); setting up the Wiesbaden Central Collecting Point (CCP) under Director Captain Walter Farmer.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 6–8</strong><br>(pp. 41–80)</td>
      <td>Captain Cooper drafts Anna as his translator for building surveys. They drive a jeep to an abandoned villa in Dotzheim. In an upstairs wardrobe, they find eight-year-old orphan Oskar Grünewald hiding with a pocket knife, bottle opener, and pencil. In the cellar, they find a secret stash of paintings. Anna identifies a large canvas by German Romantic Philipp Otto Runge (<em>The Hülsenbeck Children</em>) with a "Galerie Breuer Darmstadt" label. Cooper takes photos with his cracked Kodak camera. They take Oskar to the UNRRA DP camp, where refugee Maria Niemeyer promises to look after him.</td>
      <td>Monuments, Fine Arts, and Archives (MFAA) field work; U.S. Military Government Paragraph 51 property protections; UNRRA displaced persons camps; the September 1944 firebombing of Darmstadt.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 9–12</strong><br>(pp. 81–120)</td>
      <td>Art dealer Ludwig Schneider brings a recommendation letter from Major Phillips in Frankfurt asking for a job at the CCP. Cooper refuses because Schneider was in the SA. Anna visits Auntie Madeleine at St. Josef's hospital and brings her an egg and quince jam. Madeleine gets discharged for talking back to the head nurse. At 4:15 AM, Cooper bangs on Anna's door covered in blood. Someone ambushed him in the villa basement, stole his Colt .45 pistol, and took the Runge painting. Cooper investigates Oskar's papers and finds out Oskar was adopted through the SS <em>Lebensborn</em> program in Steinhöring, Bavaria.</td>
      <td>De-Nazification vetting of gallery dealers; the SS <em>Lebensborn</em> breeding program; black-market art trafficking; clashes between front-line MFAA officers and Frankfurt headquarters brass.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 13–15</strong><br>(pp. 121–160)</td>
      <td>Anna sees Schneider carrying a package on Schwalbacher Strasse and tails him to an apartment building with nameplates for Fromm, Schenk, and Knopf. On Saturday, Anna, Cooper, Amalia, and Oskar ride bikes to the Neroberg hill. They visit the Russian chapel and eat apples and Hershey bars. Anna confesses that her husband Thomas is a dedicated communist doctor in Thuringia. Cooper proposes a sting operation: Anna will offer a landscape painting to Schneider to catch him breaking Paragraph 51. Meanwhile, Oskar breaks down and tells Anna how his mother Magda died when their Darmstadt cellar collapsed during the 1944 bombing.</td>
      <td>Arrival of Berlin museum treasures from the Merkers salt mine; civilian fear of Soviet zone repatriation; enforcement and defiance of military non-fraternization rules.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 16–18</strong><br>(pp. 161–200)</td>
      <td>Two MPs arrest Anna at her apartment and take her to USFET headquarters for four hours of harsh questioning about Cooper's missing reports. The next evening, wearing Uncle Otto's old wool pants and wristwatch, Anna takes a Rhine landscape from the villa to the Nassauer Hof hotel. Schneider and his partner "Konrad Schenk" meet her in a bedroom. Schenk recognizes the painting instantly, exposes Anna's trap, and threatens her family. Cooper fails to show up because he saw Schneider's lookouts following Anna to the church. Schenk shoves Anna into the hallway, and she throws up on the hotel carpet.</td>
      <td>USFET military tribunals; criminal networks operating out of luxury hotels like the Nassauer Hof; forged transit documents used for black-market border crossings.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 19–20</strong><br>(pp. 201–220)</td>
      <td>Major Phillips comes to Wiesbaden, screams at Cooper for insubordination, and banishes him to count supplies at the Erbenheim airfield. Schneider takes over an office at the CCP. Frau Obersdorfer sends Anna back to the typing pool. Outside the museum fence, Schenk offers Anna unstamped travel papers from Weimar to Wiesbaden for $300, threatening to report Thomas as a communist if she refuses. Amalia invites Emil Schilling to dinner at Adolfsallee. Emil brings dark bread, cheese, and Nordhäuser schnapps, promising Anna he will protect her and Amalia from his sister.</td>
      <td>Military corruption and favoritism; forged travel permits; deep trauma and alcoholism among Wehrmacht veterans who survived Operation Barbarossa and Stalingrad.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 21–22</strong><br>(pp. 221–240)</td>
      <td>Anna sneaks into the file room and intercepts a phone call from Major Phillips' secretary, who needs an appraiser for a large painting of a girl on a chair with a torn Darmstadt label (the Runge). Anna steals an Army jeep from the museum courtyard, drives to the DP camp, and finds out Oskar ran away. At the Dotzheim villa, Anna finds $300 in cash hidden in the wardrobe, but corrupt Corporal Miller catches her and tears her blouse. Anna flees to Cooper and Corporal Bender at the airfield. Meanwhile, Amalia finds an SS <em>Helferin</em> lightning-bolt pin in a toy trunk. Frieda hits Amalia, Emil pulls Cooper's stolen gun on Frieda, and Anna arrives just in time to rescue Amalia.</td>
      <td>Military supply graft; complicity of female SS auxiliaries (<em>SS-Helferinnen</em>); underground childcare dangers in bombed cities.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><strong>Chapters 23–25</strong><br>(pp. 241–297)</td>
      <td>A formal military hearing dismisses Anna when Phillips and Schenk collude against her. At the Albrechtstrasse jail, a guard tells Anna that Emil hanged himself in his cell using his torn shirt. Emil leaves Anna his Rhine landscape painting. Anna pulls off tape on the canvas stretcher and finds Cooper's developed camera photo of the Runge painting with "Good luck, Anna" written on the back. Oskar reveals that Schenk is actually his uncle, master Nazi art looter Gerhart Heinrich of Galerie Neustadt, and that Oskar's mother Magda painted the landscapes. Anna brings the proof to Director Farmer. The MPs arrest Schneider in the workshop, Heinrich is exposed, and Miller's bribes are halted. Cooper sneaks Anna, Amalia, and Oskar in to see the bust of Queen Nefertiti. Thomas writes asking Anna to join him in the Soviet zone, but Anna refuses. Promoted to a Level-Two government worker with housing assistance, Anna chooses to stay in Wiesbaden with Amalia and adopt Oskar.</td>
      <td>Full exposure of the Heinrich and Schneider art theft ring; genuine family restitution; moral independence under Western Allied occupation; rejecting Soviet totalitarianism to protect a child's future.</td>
