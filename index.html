I. Praise, Real History, and Key Symbols

Civilian Defeat in August 1945 Wiesbaden

Most World War II novels focus on front-line battles. This book starts three months after the surrender. Defeated German civilians live in a "provisional purgatory." People eat a spoonful of lard for breakfast and brush their teeth with their fingers. Shoes have the toes cut off to make them fit longer. Women stand in long bread lines with ration stamps at Frau Hermann's bakery, and workers drink lukewarm saltwater bouillon made from cubes sent by the director's wife from America. Readers praise this raw, unvarnished look at civilian hunger, rubble trains (Trümmerbahn), and military occupation.

The Monuments Men and Merkers Mine Treasures

The novel connects directly to the real history of the U.S. Army Monuments, Fine Arts, and Archives (MFAA) section at the Wiesbaden Landesmuseum. Fifty trucks arrive from the Merkers salt mine smelling of mine salt. The crates contain real historical masterworks: Rembrandt's The Money Changer and the 3,300-year-old painted limestone bust of Queen Nefertiti. Captain Cooper secretly opens Nefertiti's crate for Anna, Amalia, and Oskar, showing them that great art endures long after dictators fall.

The Snow Queen and the Devil's Mirror

Captain Cooper gives six-year-old Amalia a copy of Hans Christian Andersen’s The Snow Queen. The fairy tale runs through the whole novel. The devil's shattered mirror represents the moral blindness of the Nazi regime, where people saw only ugliness and lies. At the end, Amalia reminds Anna that roses bloom from underneath the earth where tears fall on graves. This symbol foreshadows how love and courage can grow out of the ruins of war.

II. Character Controversies and Reader Debates

Character / Topic Core Conflict & Reader Arguments Thematic Meaning
Anna Klein's Parenting Anna worries constantly about Amalia's safety. Yet, she leaves Amalia sitting on a street bench outside the museum in 90-degree heat until the child gets sunstroke. She leaves her with Frieda Schilling without checking her past. Later, she hangs an emergency house key on a red ribbon around Amalia's neck and expects her to walk home alone. Readers debate whether Anna is a reckless mother or simply an exhausted refugee facing starvation. Shows the impossible choices facing single mothers in post-war ruins where survival requires taking terrible daily risks.
Captain Henry Cooper Cooper is an architect from Iowa who wants to do the right thing. Some readers love his optimism and his care for Amalia. Others find him reckless and bossy. He ignores military protocol, hides a secret repository of paintings at the Dotzheim villa, loses his Colt .45 sidearm, skips a formal hearing in Frankfurt, and pulls Anna into a dangerous sting at the Nassauer Hof hotel. Examines the clash between strict military bureaucracy (Major Phillips) and personal moral conviction in the field.
The Romance Subplot Readers disagree about the subtle connection between Anna and Cooper. Some enjoyed the slow-burning tension between two lonely people. Others felt the romantic hints were forced and unnecessary, arguing the stolen art mystery and civilian survival plot were strong enough on their own. Questions whether romance belongs in a story about severe war trauma and divided loyalties.
Emil Schilling's Guilt Emil is a decorated Wehrmacht veteran who lost three fingers to frostbite at Leningrad. He cannot escape the memory of an Einsatzgruppen soldier shooting a mother and her baby in a Ukrainian field. He drinks Nordhäuser schnapps to quiet his mind. He steals the Runge painting to expose the truth, attacks his unrepentant sister Frieda with a silver letter opener, and hangs himself in his jail cell. Readers debate whether Emil is a tragic hero or a guilty bystander. Explores the unbearable weight of survivor guilt and the moral destruction of ordinary German soldiers.

III. Editorial Details and Book Trivia

Continuity and Historical Glitches

Sharp readers spotted minor errors. Anna twirls a Lucky Strike cigarette by its "filter," but World War II Lucky Strikes were unfiltered. In early chapters, neighbor Frau Mueller is accidentally called Frau Bucholtz on the next page. Major Phillips is occasionally referred to as a captain.

Wiesbaden Street Names

Some readers felt bogged down by repeated mentions of local German streets—Adolfsallee, Rheinstrasse, Wilhelmstrasse, Schwalbacher Strasse, and Gustav-Freytag-Strasse—without enough map context for readers outside Germany.

The Book Title

The title The Roses Underneath appears only once in Chapter 22, during Amalia's bedtime conversation about The Snow Queen. Readers noted that the title is poetic and subtle, but never fully explained in the text.

IV. Book Club Discussion Questions

  1. The Letter from Thomas: In Chapter 25, Anna receives a letter from her husband Thomas. He is running a hospital in Kappellendorf under the Soviet Kommandant, eating fresh horsemeat, and asking her to return. Why does Anna choose to stay in Wiesbaden instead? Was this decision selfish or brave?
  2. The Power of Names: Konrad Schenk turns out to be Gerhart Heinrich, a notorious art looter who faked his death. Why was it so easy for former Nazis and SS officers to hide in plain sight after the war?
  3. Frieda and the SS Pin: Amalia finds an SS Helferin lightning-bolt pin in a dress-up trunk. How does Frieda justify her wartime past as a Lebensborn nurse? What does her character show about ordinary people who supported the regime?
  4. Oskar's Healing: Oskar begins the novel as an angry boy who wanted to bomb Allied cities. What causes him to change and trust Anna? What role do his mother's landscape paintings play in his healing?
  5. The Meaning of Restitution: Returning stolen art was not simple. Corrupt Americans like Major Phillips and Corporal Miller wanted bribes, while dealers like Ludwig Schneider stripped gallery labels. Does the novel paint an honest picture of the Allied occupation?

Cooper discovers Oskar was adopted via the SS Lebensborn program in Steinhöring, Bavaria. The SS Lebensborn program (state-sponsored breeding and Aryanization); internal military tensions between field restitution officers and Frankfurt administrative brass; illicit trafficking of looted art.
Chapters 13–15
(pp. 121–160)		 50 trucks arrive from the Merkers salt mine carrying Berlin State Museum treasures (Nefertiti, Rembrandt's The Man with the Golden Helmet). Anna tails Schneider to an apartment on Schwalbacher Str., discovering his partner "Konrad Schenk." Anna takes Oskar and Cooper on a Neroberg picnic, revealing her husband Thomas's communist militancy. Cooper proposes a sting operation to catch Schneider. The Merkers mine discovery and transfer; ideological fractures between Western Allies and the Soviet zone; post-Potsdam civilian terror of communist repatriation; non-fraternization policy defiance.
Chapters 16–18
(pp. 161–200)		 Anna is detained and interrogated at USFET HQ regarding Cooper's unauthorized actions. She attempts the sting at the Nassauer Hof with a bait landscape painting from the villa repository. "Schenk" intercepts her, identifies the painting as his own, exposes her cover, and threatens her family. Cooper fails to show after spotting Schneider's goon tailing her. USFET military counter-intelligence and tribunal authority; criminal syndicate leverage over vulnerable refugees; black-market currency manipulation and falsified transit papers.
Chapters 19–20
(pp. 201–220)		 Major Phillips relieves Cooper of CCP duty and reassigns him to counting supplies at Erbenheim airfield, clearing the way to hire Schneider. Schenk corners Anna, demanding $300 for Weimar-Wiesbaden travel papers while threatening to expose Thomas as a communist. Amalia invites Emil to dinner; Emil brings schnapps, provisions, and promises to protect Anna. Military corruption and cronyism; black-market document forgery; psychological collapse of Wehrmacht survivors of Operation Barbarossa and Stalingrad.
Chapters 21–22
(pp. 221–240)		 Anna intercepts a call to Phillips' office seeking an appraisal for the Runge painting. Sneaking a jeep, Anna visits the DP camp, discovering Oskar fled after an "uncle" visited. At the villa, Anna finds $300 in US cash hidden in the wardrobe and is assaulted by corrupt Corporal Miller. Anna flees to Cooper and Bender at the airfield. Meanwhile, Amalia finds Frieda's SS Helferin lightning-bolt pin; Emil confronts Frieda with Cooper's stolen gun, and Anna arrives just in time to rescue Amalia. Illicit soldier racketeering; institutional complicity of SS auxiliary women (SS-Helferinnen); desperate grassroots investigative work to counteract official military obstruction.
Chapters 23–25
(pp. 241–297)		 Anna is dismissed at a formal tribunal when Phillips and "Schenk" collude against her. Visiting Albrechtstrasse jail, Anna learns Emil hanged himself in his cell; he leaves her his Rhine landscape. Anna discovers Cooper's developed camera photo taped behind the canvas stretcher. Oskar reveals "Schenk" is actually his uncle, master looter Gerhart Heinrich of Galerie Neustadt, and Magda painted the landscapes. Anna presents the evidence to Director Farmer; Schneider and Heinrich are arrested. Cooper shows the children Nefertiti in secret. Thomas writes demanding Anna return to the Soviet zone, but Anna chooses freedom, staying in Wiesbaden with Amalia and taking custody of Oskar. Complete unraveling of the Heinrich/Schneider art conspiracy; restitution of family heritage; personal liberation and moral awakening under post-war reconstruction; the definitive choice of Western democratic freedom over totalitarianism.

Character Profiles

﻿

Anna Klein

Protagonist / Translator

Austrian refugee from Kappellendorf, Thuringia. Former Salzburg art apprentice. Navigates extreme trauma, single motherhood, and military bureaucracy. Evolves from passive survivalist to active investigator, refusing to return to her communist husband in the Soviet zone to secure a free future for Amalia and Oskar.

Captain Henry "Coop" Cooper

MFAA Arts Officer

Architect from Iowa. Idealistic, driven Monuments Man who prioritizes returning looted cultural treasures over careerist military protocol. Despite disciplinary demotion to Erbenheim airfield, his partnership with Anna exposes high-level corruption and recovers the stolen Runge masterwork.

Amalia Klein

Anna's 6-Year-Old Daughter

The emotional anchor of the novel. Treasures her copy of The Snow Queen, her found button necklace, and her emergency key. Unwittingly uncovers Frieda's SS past by discovering the hidden lightning-bolt pin, catalyzing the family's deliverance.

Oskar Grünewald

Displaced Minor / Key Witness

Traumatized 8-year-old orphan and SS Lebensborn adoptee. Survived the Darmstadt bombing that killed his adoptive mother Magda. Forced by his corrupt uncle Gerhart Heinrich to guard looted art at the villa, he ultimately finds salvation and a real family with Anna and Amalia.

Gerhart Heinrich ("Konrad Schenk")

Primary Antagonist

Former owner of Galerie Neustadt in Mainz and notorious ERR art looter. Faked his death to operate under the alias "Konrad Schenk." Exploited his sister Magda's death, abandoned young Oskar, and bribed USFET Major Phillips to whitewash his syndicate before being unmasked by Anna's evidence.

Ludwig Schneider

Collaborator / Black Market Dealer

Former SA appraiser who seized Jewish property in Frankfurt. Partnered with Heinrich to strip original gallery labels and claim the Dotzheim villa repository as his late wife's estate. Arrested inside the CCP workshop following Anna's photographic proof.

Emil Schilling

Tragic Anti-Hero

Decorated Wehrmacht veteran crippled by frostbite and haunting memories of Einsatzgruppen atrocities in Ukraine. Stole the Runge painting to preserve its true Jewish provenance. Following a violent confrontation with his fanatic sister Frieda, he hangs himself in jail, leaving Anna the hidden photographic key to the entire case.

Frieda Schilling

Antagonist / Lebensborn Nurse

Emil's sister. Operated an informal daycare while hiding her active past as an SS Helferin and Lebensborn breeder at the Dotzheim villa. Remained an unrepentant Nazi, aiding Heinrich's syndicate until being critically wounded by Emil with a silver letter opener.

Thomas Klein

Anna's Husband

Idealistic doctor who endured Gestapo torture before enthusiastically embracing the Soviet occupation in Kappellendorf. Appointed hospital director by the Soviet Kommandant, his letters reveal he has chosen ideological subservience over his family, cementing Anna's decision to sever ties.
